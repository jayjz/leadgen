**Proposed experiment: collect a cash fee after one independently purchased move-out clean.** Version 1, prepared October 19, 2026; not executed. Scope: Massachusetts addresses within 50 miles of Lowell, starting with Lowell and adjacent towns to reduce coordination. No assumed Facebook membership, audience, business relationship, or live provider availability.

The buyer pays an established cleaning business directly. That business completes the work and pays the operator an agreed cash acquisition fee. The operator supplies permission-based intake, an introduction and confirmation follow-up. Neither a lead nor a booking is the primary outcome. No customer funds, deposits, access codes, or worker payroll pass through the operator. Use the companion `meta-transaction-log.csv` for prospective records; it contains headers only because no fieldwork has occurred.

**Freeze these rules before the first outreach.** Maximum 21 calendar days, 16 total operator hours and $100 out-of-pocket. Track all setup, permission requests, observation, unsuccessful inquiries, complaints and reconciliation time. Stop at the first ceiling; finish obligations already accepted without accepting new work. Suggested cash reserves: $20 communication/administration, $20 incremental AI costs, $20 local travel and $40 contingency. These are ceilings, not required purchases. Use an existing phone, email, spreadsheet and AI access. No ads, website, scraper, CRM subscription or custom software.

If regulatory clarification, required registration or insurance makes this scope infeasible within those limits, stop before transactions. The scope is customer acquisition for established businesses, not domestic-worker placement or outsourced cleaning fulfillment. Classification must be confirmed; using the word “referral” is insufficient. The research report links Massachusetts guidance.

**Eligibility.** A buyer must voluntarily request an actual cleaning, identify a Massachusetts service location within the radius, need the service in 2–10 days and no later than pilot day 18, have authority to arrange access, and accept a written provider quote. Seek empty apartments or small homes with ordinary move-out cleaning, working utilities and a normal business service scope. Exclude hazardous waste, mold remediation, hoarding, pest infestation, construction work, live-in or domestic staffing, and requests for a guarantee of deposit return. Exclude friends, family and operator-funded buyers from the success count.

Do not screen on budget just to manufacture attractive conversion statistics. Log budget mismatches and scope exclusions, with reasons. The offered provider fee applies only where a provider independently quotes and accepts a job of at least $300; never add work or raise the customer's price merely to reach the threshold. Lower-priced jobs remain in the demand log and show how often the proposed economics cannot work.

**Gate 1: obtain real supply and access, days 1–4.** Identify at most ten established cleaning businesses from their own public business contact channels. The report identifies Paula's and Dyment as Lowell service advertisers, and Office & Home Cleaning in Leominster as a nearby referral-program comparator. These are research candidates, not endorsements, verified partners, or confirmed available crews. Check actual travel coverage, legal business identity, current relevant insurance evidence and references. Verify who issues quotes, supervises work and takes responsibility for rework.

Seek two written agreements for **$60 cash per new, attributable job of $300 or more, after service completion and settled customer payment**, payable within three business days. This is a proposed test price, not an observed local cash-referral rate. The provider must be willing to pay from its normal acquisition budget and judge the job profitable after labor, supplies, travel, payment costs, rework allowance and the fee. If only credits or gift cards are offered, record that as a failed cash monetization test. Do not reclassify them as cash. Do not substitute recruiting workers for recruiting businesses.

The written agreement should identify the business and operator; exact fee and qualifying invoice base; territory; exclusions; availability confirmation method; customer consent process; attribution rule; due date; refund/rework treatment; and termination. Existing clients, duplicate inquiries and previously known leads do not qualify. Agree a modest provider-supplied capacity list with timestamped validity, not a guaranteed slot the operator cannot reserve. Each job gets a new provider acceptance. No exclusive claim on the entire customer relationship and no assumed recurring commission.

Ask administrators of at most four relevant communities for permission to run a disclosed trial, aiming for two approved Facebook channels. Use only authentic eligibility for membership. Record the exact allowed conduct: a trial post, business thread, or replies to existing requests; permitted frequency; use of links; and whether private follow-up must be invited. An admin's silence is not consent. No harvested member lists, unsolicited DMs to requesters, or copied private threads. Administrator permission does not authorize platform scraping.

If two providers will not agree to cash terms, stop the proposed model. If fewer than two Facebook communities approve by day 4, report access friction. One approved community can support a single-transaction feasibility attempt within the original ceilings, but cannot pass the two-community continuation gate. No approved community means stop the Meta-channel test; do not silently replace it with Reddit or the operator's friends.

**Gate 2: observe and fulfill, through day 18.** Stop new intake by day 16, complete work by day 18, and reserve days 19–21 and at least two operator hours for settlement and reconciliation. Use two brief scheduled native-interface checks per day, within the time ceiling, plus authorized inbound notifications. Before beginning, record which channels, time windows and permitted post formats will be used. Log every relevant request encountered, including already-solved requests, duplicates, out-of-area jobs, unsuitable scope, no response and prices below the qualifying threshold. De-duplicate cross-posted requests. Search-engine historical examples in the report are not prospects.

After a permitted public reply or approved trial post, the buyer must initiate or explicitly consent to follow-up. Explain the provider-paid fee before making a recommendation. Ask once for service date, approximate size, rooms/bathrooms, empty/occupied status, appliances/add-ons, parking/access constraints, budget range and preferred contact method. Let the buyer provide the minimum necessary photos voluntarily. AI can convert this into a checklist and identify missing information; a human verifies it.

Get permission to share the brief with named businesses. Initially share only the town/ZIP and job requirements; the exact address can go directly to the selected provider. Obtain up to two written, comparable quotes with scope, exclusions, total price, tax if applicable, service window, cancellation terms and responsibility for rework. The provider sets the price and confirms availability; the buyer chooses and contracts directly. No pressure based on alleged legal cleaning requirements or manufactured scarcity.

Confirm arrival arrangements the previous day. The provider handles access details directly with the buyer. After completion, obtain the buyer's acknowledgment, the provider invoice and evidence of settled customer payment with unrelated details redacted. Invoice the agreed operator fee. Count success only after that fee settles. If the buyer withdraws or the service fails, record it; do not substitute a lead fee.

**Evidence that makes a transaction count.** Use one event ID throughout:

1. Timestamped source permalink and permitted-channel record, plus the buyer's opt-in and commission disclosure acknowledgment.
2. Pre-introduction confirmation from the provider that this is a new customer/inquiry; note any prior relationship or simultaneous direct approach.
3. Buyer-accepted written provider quote with scope, full price and service date.
4. Provider completion record and buyer confirmation that the agreed work occurred; record any dispute or rework.
5. Provider invoice matched to a settled processor payout or bank receipt. A “paid” badge on an invoice alone is insufficient. For cash, obtain a dated receipt and both parties' independent acknowledgment, and report the weaker verification.
6. Operator fee invoice matched to the operator's settled bank/processor receipt. Record fees, refunds, gift cards and credits separately.

Never collect complete card numbers or account credentials. Store only redacted evidence and references necessary for attribution, consent and bookkeeping. Keep any access instructions out of the research log. Agree lawful retention and deletion; do not create a resaleable contact database.

**What attribution means.** A source code and a new-customer check establish a traceable introduction; they do not prove the buyer would otherwise have failed to transact. At intake, ask what the buyer had already tried and why they accepted help. At completion, ask which concrete step helped: finding available capacity, resolving scope, translating, comparing quotes or confirming a schedule. Record “nothing beyond another recommendation” when that is the answer. Do not claim causal lift from this small uncontrolled pilot.

Record three separate totals: customer service spend, provider fee paid to operator, and operator net cash. Only the second is operator revenue. A hypothetical $300 clean with a $60 cash referral fee is **$60 operator revenue**, not $300. If direct costs are $5 and total attributable time is 45 minutes, labor-adjusted contribution at $30/hour is $32.50 before shared acquisition/setup costs. If the same transaction requires three hours, it loses $35. Both examples are assumptions.

For the repeatable model, calculate:

`net cash per hour = (cash fees actually collected - refunds - all incremental cash costs) / all ongoing operator hours`

Also show a full-pilot version including every setup hour. Do not divide only by time spent on winners. For each provider, separately test whether its realized contribution remains positive after the fee. No implied lifetime value without repeat orders and an actual fee agreement.

**Precommitted decisions.**

| Observation | Decision |
|---|---|
| At least one completed job, settled customer payment, and settled operator cash fee by day 21, acquired through a permitted Meta channel | The first-transaction mechanism passes; this alone does not validate a business |
| At least three such jobs from unrelated buyers by day 21, across at least two permitted Facebook communities and two providers; no unresolved service complaints; each provider still wants more at the same terms | Permit a second, still-manual cohort within a separately agreed budget |
| In that second cohort, at least ten completed paid jobs, positive provider economics, and at least $30/hour operator net cash over **all ongoing work**, without introductory subsidies | Evidence for a small operating business; still not a proven moat |
| Buyer payments occur but no cash operator fee settles | Brokerage monetization failed; customer demand alone is insufficient |
| At most fifteen consecutive in-scope, opted-in buyer inquiries are worked and zero qualifying transactions complete; or day/hour/cash cap is reached without one | Stop the current offer. Report exact funnel and missing outcomes |
| Few or no inquiries appear within the permitted access/time budget | Insufficient accessible throughput; do not infer zero underlying market demand |
| Providers have no spare profitable capacity, quotes consistently exceed buyer budgets, or free recommendations solve nearly everything first | Abandon the demand-matching mechanism in this segment |
| Buyers pay for physical cleaning, lifting, storage or handover attendance, but not coordination | A different local service may be viable; this does not validate AI distribution arbitrage |
| Work comes only through non-Meta referrals, or existing provider clients | Potential ordinary referral/administrative service; Meta-specific thesis remains unproven |
| Account restriction, withdrawn permission, disputed data use, unsafe scope or unresolved legal classification | Stop the affected activity immediately; complete or responsibly unwind existing obligations |

Three $60 fees produce just $180 revenue. At the full 16-hour and $100 ceiling, that is $80 net cash and $5/hour before taxes: a deliberately small learning expense, not economic success. The first test can pass mechanically while the investment thesis fails economically.

**Test the AI contribution separately.** In a later manual cohort, randomly assign suitable intake briefs to human-only drafting or AI-assisted drafting with the same human checks, quotes and service standards. Include review/correction time. Compare total minutes, quote corrections and misunderstandings, not word count. Ten jobs only give preliminary directional evidence. If AI saves no time after checking, remove it. If one repetitive task still consumes at least two hours weekly over four weeks and paid volume persists, evaluate an authorized integration. Access barriers, lack of customers and insufficient capacity are not software requirements.

**Prepared messages — not sent.** Fill factual blanks only after verification.

Provider approach: “I'm testing a small local customer-introduction service around Lowell. You would quote, contract, perform and invoice under your own business. I'm proposing $60 cash only after a new customer completes and pays for a job of at least $300. I would disclose the fee to the buyer. Would you review a short trial agreement and confirm whether you have profitable capacity? No paid leads or subscription.”

Administrator request: “May I run a small, clearly disclosed trial helping residents obtain scoped move-out cleaning quotes from local businesses? Businesses would pay me a fee after completed paid work. I would follow your allowed post/reply format, and contact residents privately only if invited. There would be no member-list collection or automated group monitoring. Please specify whether this is allowed and under what conditions.”

Buyer disclosure: “I coordinate introductions to local cleaning businesses. If you hire a participating business and the work is completed and paid, it pays me $60; you do not pay me a separate fee. It sets the quote and is responsible for the work. You can compare or book independently. With your permission, I can share your job brief with [named businesses].”

Quote confirmation: “The quote is from [business]: [total], for [scope], on [date/window], with [exclusions and cancellation terms]. Please confirm directly with the business. Availability is confirmed only when it accepts your booking.”

No statement claims personal experience, insurance verification, a guaranteed result or a held slot unless the operator has supporting evidence. Outreach must be performed only with the user's authorization and the applicable platform/community permissions. The present deliverable is the protocol and reusable records, not an executed field test.
