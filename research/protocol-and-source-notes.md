Research date: October 19, 2026. This is a desk-research record and prospective protocol. No buyers were contacted, no experiment was run, and no payments were observed. No lead-generation software was built. Historical records below are closed examples, not current leads.

**Decision and scope.** Test whether Pennsylvania project-specific prevailing-wage determinations expose standalone public-school structured-cabling procurements early enough to improve qualified contractors' bidding participation. Sell original, source-linked procurement research to existing cabling contractors; the school buys installation and the contractor buys the research. An exclusive or overlooked source has not been established. The state's public-search publication delay remains the main unresolved assumption.

**Clock discipline.** Record four separate clocks: determination creation, first public availability, first discovery by the target contractor, and the last action needed to remain eligible. A board award date can follow practical vendor selection. Spring Grove's determination preceded its last listed survey by 23 days, bid deadline by 45, intended selection by 55, and stated board-award date by 90. None is a measured public information advantage. Do not label a date embedded in a PDF or a search engine crawl date as first publication.

For prospective monitoring, store the last successful check in which a record was absent and the first successful check in which it was present. Public appearance is interval-censored between them. A failed query is missing data, not proof of absence. Search overlapping prior dates for backfills and amendments. Use conservative interval bounds when asserting that an advantage exceeds three business days.

**One prospective experiment.** Geography: Pennsylvania statewide. Record: newly appearing project-specific wage determinations. Work: standalone public-school copper/fiber structured-cabling installation, not full-building electrical packages, repairs under an existing vendor contract, road fiber or hardware-only orders. Customer: established cabling installers already qualified for school procurement and, when relevant, E-rate. Verify service territory, references, SPIN and bid capacity first.

Offer a six-week, $350 paid manual monitoring pilot: source-linked early notices, project-fit assessment, required visit/deadline calendar and amendment follow-up. Price is an experimental ask, not a market observation. Explain that volume and awards are uncertain and that the comparison includes ordinary-timing notices. Refunds are deducted from receipts. No commission or claim of access to decision-makers.

Week 1: manually verify search usability against the three serials; record source terms, access costs and extraction time. Ask L&I about posting cadence and existing timestamp documentation, if the operator executes the experiment. If public retrieval cannot be reproduced cheaply, stop. Do not build a scraper to conceal the problem.

Recruit at most 12 qualified contractor firms through their public business contacts, with one personalized message and one follow-up. Ask for a recent actual procurement they missed, its original alert dates, the binding deadline, bid-preparation hours, and the reason they did not bid. Seek three unrelated paid customers. If none pay, stop. One or two is inconclusive and does not meet the continuation gate. Separately interview up to six officers on closed school procurements about actual selection timing, bidders and reasons for disqualification; request supporting public documents. During open bids use formal questions only, not private vendor-preference interviews.

For up to 12 weeks, review new statewide determinations every business day and recheck a rolling 30-day window. Filter school agencies and terms including structured cabling, network cabling, Cat6/Cat6A, data cabling and fiber. Read scope rather than matching generic electrical jobs. Log every candidate, exclusion, duplicate, canceled project, failed query and nonresponse. The first 10 consecutive relevant records are the publication-timing sample; retain late/ineligible records in this denominator. An existing record at enrollment cannot retrospectively prove early appearance.

Gate: at least 6/10 must have a conservatively established advantage of at least three business days over the earlier of the official solicitation/Form 470 or the participating contractor's documented existing alert, with at least seven calendar days before the last mandatory eligibility action and no evidence of prior selection. Unknown posting times do not pass. If this gate fails, terminate the early-information claim regardless of willingness to pay for document summarization.

Accumulate 12 distinct eligible district projects, grouped into six matched pairs by contractor fit, expected scope, timing and travel distance. Use different districts and, where possible, different procurement administrators. Freeze pairs and qualification before outcomes. Assign one project in each pair by a recorded coin flip to early delivery; deliver the other at ordinary public-solicitation timing. Packet depth is identical; clients keep their normal alerts. Assign each procurement to only one participating client and never disclose competitors' bids. Log prior awareness and contamination, analyze as randomized, and show a sensitivity analysis including affected cases.

Primary outcome: a timely responsive bid with required site attendance, evidenced by a submission receipt and later buyer confirmation or bid tabulation. Raw contact/reply counts are not the primary endpoint. A strong small-pilot continuation threshold is at least five pairs where only the early arm produces the outcome and no pair favoring control. Under an independent paired randomization sharp null, five concordantly directed discordant pairs have one-sided sign probability 1/32; this is a screening test for a large effect, not proof of repeatable revenue or a generalizable conversion rate. If too few distinct projects or pairs appear within 12 weeks, report insufficient throughput and stop without broadening the segment after seeing results.

Record outcomes through award and, for up to nine months, cash collection. A documented chain requires record -> delivered notice -> previously unaware qualified contractor -> required action -> accepted bid -> named award -> invoice -> bank receipt. Obtain permission to reconcile contractor receipts with contract/FRN identifiers. USAC commitments or authorized disbursements are supporting evidence, not a substitute for contractor bank receipts. Keep operator research fees and contractor installation revenue in separate columns. One attributable win is process evidence; randomization is what supplies the counterfactual for bidding participation.

Agent responsibility: extract candidate fields, flag inconsistencies, draft match suggestions and evidence summaries. Human responsibility: operate source access during validation, verify every field against documents, decide qualification, conduct interviews, approve and send messages, handle prices/contracts and verify receipts. No automatic outreach, pricing, bid submission or funding certifications.

Ceilings: $300 out-of-pocket, 40 operator hours for the 12-week acquisition/intervention, plus four hours total for later outcome checks. Suggested cash allocation: $60 communication/address costs, $50 AI/OCR, $40 document access, $150 contingency. Time allocation: 10 hours source checking, 10 hours document verification, 8 hours recruitment/interviews, 8 hours packets/outcome logging, 4 hours analysis. If actual portal workload exceeds this, stop. Track learning/setup time separately from recurring delivery time, but disclose both.

Proceed to a second manual cohort only if the timing gate, bidding gate, three independent paid customers, two renewals at the same price, and at least one independently verified contractor award are met. Require positive recurring contribution after cash costs and operator time valued at $50/hour. This hourly value is a decision rule, not a claim about market wages. Do not automate before documented contractor cash collection and a second cohort replicate the useful timing effect. If customers pay but the source is no earlier, label any continuation as a separate procurement-research service; it does not validate this mission's information advantage. Stop for no payments, no earlier discovery, inaccessible sources, insufficient volume, systematic preselection or negative delivery economics.

**Evidence discipline.** Publish aggregate positive and negative results, the frozen inclusion criteria, exclusion counts, assignment log, actual hours/costs and missingness. Do not publish confidential bid prices before lawful disclosure. Current evidence proves record existence and one documented procurement outcome; public lead time, signal precision, competitive novelty, willingness to pay, incrementality and repeatability remain unverified.
